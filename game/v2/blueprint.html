Hostile Inference — the laboratory race

Version 0.2, 10 October 2026. C's ceramic architectural world is accepted. The original military roster is superseded by this laboratory ecosystem. This document separates implemented review tools from proposed tactical game rules.

A short tactical showcase now exists at /demo/index.html. See docs/server-continents-v0.4.md for the implemented encounter, capture controls and scope. The broad campaign systems below remain design proposals except where marked implemented.

The world

The Littoral Protocol is a coastal research region built around clean power, cold water, university talent, chip fabrication and submarine cables. Three laboratories compete to control the next frontier model. They also depend on institutions they do not own. A chip foundry wants contracts fulfilled. A university wants researchers retained and its data respected. A water trust wants the estuary to survive. A benchmark observatory wants results that can be reproduced.

Models do not replace every human and institution in the world. Research still depends on people, hardware still travels on ships and rail, and an apparently limitless cloud has very physical pumps and transformers. The satire lives in the doctrines and incidents; the strategic rules take those dependencies seriously.

An army has three connected layers. Physical units include people, battery logistics, ships, campuses and ports. Network units travel along actual endpoint and cable links: malware cannot simply walk over a hill and infect an isolated archive. Embodied models inhabit portable inference shells; the abstract ceramic form is their readable game body, tethered to compute through a network. Disconnected models have a short local buffer before losing capability. A virus and a cooling ship therefore use different movement rules and different routes.

The central loop

Access to energy and cooling enables compute. Compute is shared between inference income, staffed research, security and active model units. Clean datasets and active researchers turn research compute into useful releases. Verified releases earn trust, unlock contracts and improve the model roster. Income funds infrastructure, staff and logistics. Every offensive system attacks a link in that loop.

The player makes visible tradeoffs: run profitable contracts or train the next model; spend chips on a fleet or retain recovery capacity; lock down a compromised link or accept congestion; buy clean data or scrape faster and risk trust; launch an unverified model now or let a rival finish first.

Factions

Misanthropic — proof before power

Warm clay, oat and cocoa; ivory structures with protected workspaces and layered containment. It builds an operationally conservative ecosystem around verified datasets, audits and local fallback models. It can survive compromised data better, but loses time to its own review process and needs more staff to make that process useful.

Its initial sandbox modifier is 12% slower research and a smaller initial quality loss from poisoning. The tactical proposal extends this to stronger quarantine and containment. This is a proposed balance trade, not evidence of a solved matchup.

Closed AI — every future has a licence

White ceramic, emerald interface accents and charcoal sealed cores. Its advantage is a network of efficient inference contracts, narrow specialisation and precise access control. Its weakness is dependence on proprietary access and central cloud fabric. Local offline forks are a real strategic hedge rather than a cosmetic unlock.

Its initial sandbox modifier is 12% more inference income and a larger contract penalty during a cable/access interruption. Its tactical abilities concentrate power inside a protected corridor instead of defending an entire map.

Broke Labs — launch, learn, apologise

Graphite, white and chrome, with diagnostic red reserved for failures. It wins timing windows with faster releases, recruitment disruption and opportunistic hardware. It must then manage heat, patch debt and public trust. The fun is taking a calculated operational risk, not receiving free speed with no downside.

Its initial sandbox modifier is 15% faster research and 18% more heat per served compute. Low-quality releases lose more trust. In the tactical proposal, burst abilities have a visible cooldown and a recovery period that opponents can exploit.

Specialists that deepen the world

These nine specialist roles have designed rules but do not yet have their own illustrated production assets.

  • Provenance Auditor: verifies a captured archive and removes hidden contamination. It needs a safe work window; it cannot clean a hostile link instantly.
  • Distillation Courier: carries compact checkpoints. Intercepting one enables a weaker fork, not the entire opponent’s research tree.
  • Scraper Flock: explores public archives for rare data. Exhausting a district and violating its cooperative contract create trust and quality costs.
  • Eval Judge: submits a model to the neutral benchmark observatory. Independent reruns prevent fake leaderboard victories.
  • Incident Responder: repairs queues, contains escaped models and returns staff to locked laboratories. It is vulnerable while working.
  • Talent Scout: recruits rare researchers through influence at a university rather than offensive poaching. Staff commons and retention funding counter recruitment pressure.
  • Chip Convoy: physical hardware moves through road, rail and shipping. A funded data centre gains no new capacity until the convoy arrives.
  • Cable Tender: repairs and builds alternative offshore routes. The sea becomes an infrastructure network, not an empty border.
  • Open-weight Fork: a weaker local model keeps essential work running during a proprietary licence failure or cable cut.

The civilian navy

Naval play is a second economic layer. Cooling vessels deliver water, recover overloaded barges and deploy heat exchanges. Floating clouds move compute and create offshore expansion sites. Chip freighters and cable tenders connect foundries, ports and campuses. Science vessels explore rare datasets in remote observatories.

The tactical proposal uses berths, shipping lanes, finite coolant reserves and cable connection points. Naval sabotage targets access, pumps and links. A ship losing access to its cable may keep a small local workload running, while its campus must reroute the rest. Ordinary logistics ships do not acquire cannons merely because the game needs combat. Escorts are security agents, scan nodes and models that contest network access.

Buildings and the human pipeline

Twelve original structure concepts cover four buildings for each faction: laboratory campus, research laboratory, inference centre and cooling exchange. Each has a visible operating dependency and failure state.

Shared structure roles extend that foundation: grid exchange, dataset conservatory, agent bureau, containment ward, dry dock, cloud fabric exchange, chip depot and staff commons. Models unlock through research and validation. Agents unlock through a staffed bureau. Naval infrastructure needs a viable shoreline and cable landing. Cooling upgrades cannot be built in a completely waterless district without a transport plan.

Researchers occupy workstations, not an invisible population count. A lab can be standing and idle because its staff are locked out. A researcher can stop ordinary training to perform an audit. Burnout grows through prolonged overwork and clears through recovery, housing and healthy staff rotations. Poaching is a timed conversion of output with visible counterplay, not a permanent instant theft of the entire unit.

Three larger maps

Delta Commons — 128 × 128 proposed tactical cells

An expansive archipelago with three campuses, an independent foundry and railway, university commons, renewables basin, central benchmark observatory, protected wetlands and cable harbour. Ground transport, bridges, water routes and cables produce multiple fronts. Offshore data centres are useful only when their logistics and links survive.

Cold Storage — 128 × 160 proposed tactical cells

A polar fjord where free cold is plentiful but reliable power is scarce. Geothermal access, chip shipping and a narrow cable corridor matter more than owning another chiller. Ice closures change route options; they must not randomly eliminate the only starting energy path.

Windward Sprawl — 160 × 128 proposed tactical cells

A semi-arid solar frontier with abundant daytime power, scarce cooling water, a shared reservoir, urban cloud exchange, university and foundry. Storage, water efficiency and offshore cooling decide the long game. Solar peaks and heat waves create timing windows rather than arbitrary economic failure.

These dimensions describe intended playable footprints. The three delivered illustrations are world concepts, not generated navigation masks or implemented 16,384/20,480-cell maps. Exact passability, unit scale and route fairness remain tactical production work.

For two-player and three-player modes, produce separate simulation layouts. Compare time to a first home delivery, first clean dataset, first expansion, benchmark access, opponent approach and naval foothold from every spawn. Spawn positions cannot be chosen from artwork alone. Tactical data determines collision and routes; the artwork must follow that data.

Neutral institutions and map politics

The independent foundry limits chip throughput. University commons supply staff and verified data. Data cooperatives offer finite licensed datasets. Water trusts grant sustainable intake contracts. Grid brokers sell charging priority. Benchmark venues verify capability. Small open-weight communities provide fallback models.

Relationships are expressed through contracts, prices, access and environmental state. Trust gives opportunity; it does not act as an extra health bar that instantly destroys a faction. Institutions can favour a reliable customer, close an exhausted resource or request incident response. The player can contest access without every neutral institution being a capture flag.

Tactical ranges, channel times, cooldowns, integrity damage and resistance classes must be explicit. Agents reveal themselves after an action and have an interruptible channel. Malware requires an exposed link; a clean air gap prevents a jump. Model attacks consume compute and cannot fire for free during a full brownout. A fortified faction can be outmanoeuvred by split logistics pressure, not just defeated by bigger numbers.

Match objectives

The primary proposed mode is Frontier Run. Complete three independently verified research milestones, then sustain a final public inference trial for 120 seconds. The trial needs useful output, adequate staffing and a trusted dataset. Rival pressure pauses progress but does not erase an earned milestone. The race therefore demands both scientific progress and operational survival.

Operational defeat is the alternative: a faction loses after it has no working campus or connected floating-cloud foothold, no restart-capable staff, and no recovery route for a 90-second grace period. A lone powerless building is not an indefinite stalemate. Supply interruption alone does not instantly eliminate a player who has a credible restart path.

Campaign proposals include a shared-water crisis, a chip shortage, an escaped model that crosses networks, and a neutral benchmark scandal. These scenarios are fiction about the game’s institutions, not factual allegations about real laboratories.

Research and progression

The initial technology paths are Operations, Model Science and Field Systems. Operations unlocks storage, recirculation, scheduler efficiency and cable redundancy. Model Science unlocks provenance, specialisation, distillation, interpretability and frontier evals. Field Systems unlocks agents, containment, embodied models and offshore clouds.

Unlocks consume staffed work and compute, and require a data foundation. A rushed release can create an earlier weaker unit, operational debt and lower trust. A verified release takes longer but makes contracts and recovery more resilient. Public benchmark control speeds verification; it is useful without being the sole possible victory path.

The implemented systems lab

The current browser contains a deterministic operating sandbox with finite compute, energy throughput, cooling, research staff, quality, trust, paid support deployment and model training. Its UI exposes the dependency graph, current bottlenecks, thermal history, accounting and operation log. This is a causal prototype, not tactical combat or a complete skirmish.

Initial state: 1,600 funds, 96 local compute, 112 energy supply, 105 cooling capacity, 12 researchers, one released model, 94 data quality and 78 trust. Allocations are 42 commercial inference, 26 research and 12 security; running units consume 8 additional compute. Heat is a dimensionless 0–100 gameplay index, not a physical temperature forecast.

The scheduler shares capacity when total demand exceeds effective compute. A power shortfall multiplies throughput. Heat above 65 reduces compute before complete shutdown. Researchers and clean data determine training rate. Inference income depends on served compute, data quality, trust and faction modifiers.

Training reserves 220 funds. Cancelling returns the fraction of unperformed work. A completed model adds 5 ongoing compute demand and changes trust depending on quality. This training-checkpoint price is intentionally different from the proposed cost of deploying a fully embodied escaped-model unit.

Support actions cost money: energy logistics 300, cooling vessel 480, one recruited researcher 180, commissioned data centre 680, floating cloud 1,100, audit 240 and incident response 320. The sandbox has 12 research workstations; surplus staff create lockout resilience rather than unlimited free training speed. New data-centre capacity waits for a 25-second abstract chip delivery. Floating-cloud capacity is lost while its cable is cut. Income and thermal state continue during these waits.

Eight test incidents cover blackouts, drought, poisoning, researcher lockout, viral demand, model escape, cable cut and leaked weights. Infection does not disappear just because time passes. Security slows quality loss and accelerates access/containment recovery; an audit actually removes infection. The appropriate countermeasure matters: buying another cooling vessel does not repair a grid interruption.

The simulation pauses when hidden or when leaving its tab. Switching factions or Reset starts a new scenario. The +30 seconds button executes 30 one-second simulation ticks; running mode uses bounded 0.1-second ticks. No real service, model account, external API, cyber payload or paid provider is involved.

Art and production boundary

The delivered atlases contain 24 original static entity concepts and 12 original static buildings with real alpha. They share C's fixed isometric ceramic camera and lighting. Three original larger world concepts complete the visual layer. Existing art is retained as an earlier exploration, not presented as the active army.

The atlases still need native sprite-size review, stable pivots, individual source rectangles, low-alpha fringe cleanup, facing/animation states, overlays and an authored navigation map before serving as production game atlases. The nine specialists and eight shared structure roles need dedicated art. Preview SVG viewboxes preserve original pixel proportions; they do not manufacture animation states.

Validation and balance plan

The current tests can establish accounting, bounds, deterministic state, delayed deliveries, staffing dependence, thermal response and meaningful recovery. They cannot establish faction win-rate balance or tactical fairness because there are no tactical opponents here.

The playable stage must add separate physical/network navigation layers, visible link reach, command queues, staff workstation assignment, legitimate opponent information, full production and deployment, the Frontier Run objective, operational defeat and save/load. Then run mirror matches, every faction pairing, swapped spawns, naval/land compositions, different opponent policies and resource-denial recovery scenarios. Human playtests must confirm that each failure has a understandable warning and a possible response.

Do not claim balance based only on a neat cost table. Publish matchup counts, seeds, map layouts, policies and observed limitations.
